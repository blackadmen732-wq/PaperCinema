# Research: the duel at Ganryūjima (1612)

This film follows the most famous duel of Miyamoto Musashi. The story is real, but most details come from tradition written down long afterwards. The film marks which is which.

## Confidence levels used in this project

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Documented** | Sources agree, and it isn't seriously disputed |
| **Tradition** | Told in the classic accounts, but the sources are late or disagree |
| **Our choice** | Artistic decision for the film, not from a source |

## Facts and how the film uses them

| What | Status | Notes | In the film |
|---|---|---|---|
| Musashi fought Sasaki Kojirō on a small island in the Kanmon Strait (between Honshu and Kyushu) and Kojirō died | **Documented** | This is the one point every source treats as settled. The island, Funashima, was later called *Ganryūjima* after Kojirō's school, Ganryū. | Whole film |
| Date: 13 April 1612 | **Tradition** | Most commonly given, but sources differ: some give 12 April 1612, one gives 1610. | Title card says "April 1612 (traditional date)" |
| The island is small, low and flat | **Documented** (geography) | Still true today, though it was later enlarged by land reclamation. | Low sandy island with a few coastal pines |
| Musashi arrived late, by boat | **Tradition** | One version says he misjudged the strong, complicated tides. | S3 |
| Musashi carved a wooden sword from a spare oar | **Tradition** | Told in the classic account. Its length is usually given as about 110 cm, longer than Kojirō's sword. | His weapon throughout |
| Kojirō's sword was unusually long (about 3 shaku ≈ 91 cm blade), nicknamed *Monohoshizao* ("the laundry pole") | **Tradition** | Well known, but no surviving sword is proven to be it. | Our paper sword is an original design based on this |
| Kojirō threw his scabbard into the sea, and Musashi said "Kojirō, you have already lost" (you wouldn't throw away the scabbard if you expected to sheathe your sword again) | **Tradition** | Classic and widely repeated. The film labels it "as tradition tells it". | S4 |
| Musashi's headband was cut by Kojirō's blade | **Tradition** | Part of the classic telling. | S5: the headband tail flies off |
| One blow from the wooden sword to Kojirō's head | **Tradition** | Accounts differ on where and how. | S5 |
| Musashi left by boat right afterwards | **Tradition** | | S6 |
| Kojirō's age | **Disputed** | The *Nitenki* says 18, which conflicts with other evidence. It may come from a misread character in the interview notes. | Not stated in the film |
| Kojirō's red jacket | **Our choice** | The crimson colour echoes your kimono reference image. Popular versions (for example Yoshikawa's 1935 novel) also give him bright clothes, but this isn't historical evidence. | Kojirō's costume |

## Main sources and their limits

- **Nitenki (二天記), 1776.** This is the main source for the duel story, written about 130 years after Musashi died (1645). It's based on family and retainer interviews (it builds on the earlier *Musashi Kō Den* and the *Kensetsu* notes). Treat its details as tradition.
- **Musashi's own writing (*Gorin no Sho*, about 1645)** says he fought more than 60 duels and never lost, but it doesn't describe Ganryūjima.
