# The film's sword: "Shiokaze" (潮風, "sea wind")

## What you sent

Your three reference swords: **Enkaryū** (black and red fantasy zanpakutō), **Ame no Habakiri V2** (glowing cursed fantasy blade), and a **black long blade with a black lacquer scabbard**. They share a mood: black lacquer, deep red accents, metal fittings, a long dramatic blade.

As you asked, I didn't copy any of them. I designed a new sword that keeps that mood but where **every part is something that existed on real swords around 1600–1615**. That's the period of the duel, the start of what sword historians call the *Shintō* ("new sword") era.

## Who carries it

Kojirō. Tradition says his sword was unusually long (about 3 shaku), so a long blade like your references fits him historically. Musashi carries his oar-carved wooden sword, as the story says.

## Every part, and why it's accurate

| Part | Japanese term | Our design | Historical basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blade length | nagasa | 91 cm (3 shaku) | Kojirō's traditional blade length. Normal katana of the time were about 70 cm, so this is a long sword. |
| Curve | sori | 2.7 cm, curved so the edge side is outward | A real katana is curved with the edge on the outside of the curve. |
| Width | mihaba | 3.3 cm at the base, narrowing to 2.4 cm | A normal taper for the period. |
| Temper line | hamon | A white paper strip with a gently wavy (notare/gunome) border | A wavy hamon is typical of early Shintō smiths. |
| Blade collar | habaki | Copper | Copper habaki were standard. |
| Guard | tsuba | Round, dark iron, with wave-shaped cut-outs | Iron openwork tsuba (sukashi) were the common style for fighting swords of the period, as were the two side holes for small tools (kogai/kozuka). The waves refer to the strait where the duel happened. This is an **original design**, not a copy of a known tsuba. |
| Handle wrap | tsuka-ito | Black silk wrap with white ray-skin diamonds showing | Ray skin (samegawa) under silk wrap is how real handles were made. White skin showing through the diamonds is accurate. (Enkaryū's red diamonds aren't.) |
| Handle ornaments | menuki | Small gold pieces under the wrap | Standard. |
| Collar and end cap | fuchi / kashira | Plain iron | A plain, practical style like the Higo mountings of the early 1600s. |
| Scabbard | saya | Black lacquer (kuro-urushi), plain | Black lacquer was the most common scabbard finish. It's the link to your 3rd reference. |
| Mouth and tip | koiguchi / kojiri | Dark horn at the mouth, iron at the tip | Real materials of the period. |
| Cord | sageo | **Deep red** cord tied through the side knob (kurikata) | Coloured cords were normal, and this is where your red accent from Enkaryū lives, accurately. |
| Total length | | About 1.24 m | Long blade plus a long handle (33 cm) for a two-handed grip. |

## What I left out on purpose (not historical)

Glowing blades, flame patterns on the scabbard, a spiked tsuba, a red-glowing edge (from Ame no Habakiri), and the dragon spirit. These are fantasy, and you asked for historical fact. They could come back later as a separate "stylised" version if you want one.
